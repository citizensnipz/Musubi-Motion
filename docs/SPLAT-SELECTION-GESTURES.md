# Lasso and paint selection

Select one splat object and open **Select → Splat Components** or press **F8**. Choose **Lasso** or **Paint Selection** in the docked panel. **Q** returns to Click / Box, and **F8** returns to Object mode. The selection count and center highlights update together when a gesture finishes. The viewport shows the closed lasso outline or painted coverage while you drag. Escape, pointer loss, mode changes, and changes to the pose or camera cancel the gesture or its scan and retain the previous selection. Wait for the count after release before beginning the next stroke. A new gesture supersedes a pending scan.

- **Lasso** closes the last point to the first. Its even-odd fill selects both lobes of a self-crossing outline. Centers on the outline count as hits. Short and collinear traces leave the selection as it was.
- **Paint Selection** uses a circular cursor with an adjustable radius of 1–256 CSS pixels. Stroke spacing is measured along the path and ranges from 1 px up to the radius. The first and last brush dabs are always included. Each original index contributes at most once per stroke, including when the path doubles back. Use **B** plus horizontal drag to resize the radius in the viewport, or type or middle-drag the values in the panel. Escape during resizing restores the previous radius.

Lasso replaces the selection by default. The brush has an explicit **Operation** setting with Add as the default, alongside Replace, Subtract and Toggle. For either tool, **Shift** toggles, **Ctrl** subtracts, and **Ctrl+Shift** adds. On macOS, Command substitutes for Ctrl. Modifiers are read when the gesture starts and take precedence over the selected brush operation.

Both tools use the [original-center projection and depth rules](SPLAT-SELECTION.md). Through selects across depth, and Front centers keeps the nearest eligible center in each 4 CSS pixel cell within the gesture. Source opacity and evaluated visibility apply before projection. Membership uses original asset indices, and gestures and settings stay outside scene history and native archives.

Scans read at most 4,096 source positions per batch and yield between batches. Lasso compares edges in vertical screen bands, and brush compares dabs in radius-sized screen cells. A gesture can contain up to 8,192 lasso samples or brush dabs. Excessive traces, coverage work exceeding 12 million candidate checks, and results over the existing one-million-ID limit report an error and preserve the previous selection. Rendering still samples up to 20,000 selected center markers.

Generate the original 87-record fixture with `node scripts/generate-splat-gesture-fixture.mjs`, then import `examples/splat-gesture-regions.splat` and choose Front view. It has two aligned 25-center layers on the left, a 25-center layer on the right, an 11-center curved row below, and one transparent center. Use the left layers to compare Through and Front centers, or follow the curved row with the brush.

The tool mapping follows Maya's closed, accurate lasso and Paint Selection settings, including **B** plus drag to resize and **Q** to return to the default tool. Musubi puts the tool selector and radius controls in its existing Splat Components panel. The brush shows coverage during the drag and updates membership after the scan on release. Browser focus preserves native text editing. The overlay artwork and procedural fixture are original. The tools reuse the installed Three.js 0.180.0 and Spark 2.4.0 APIs under their MIT licenses.
